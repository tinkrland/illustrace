# palette research

sources from the artschool research pass (tavily + firecrawl, 2026-09-20). research proposes, images dispose.

## notan

the strongest find. notan (japanese light-dark harmony) is a composition tradition that works with exactly two values (strict) or three (liberal, black-white-mid), used in composition studies precisely because stripping color and extra values reveals "the underlying value structure" of a picture (will kemp art school, mitch albala, valuestudy.app). it survived into this folder's axes because it is rare in color vocabulary: a concept that is simultaneously observable (count the tonal steps), nameable by users, and pre-validated by a century of teaching practice. the value_structure axis is basically the notan ladder extended with full_range at the top.

## color schemes

the classical scheme names (mono, analogous, complementary, triadic, split-complementary) came back as stable across every source, which is expected: they are geometric, definitional, and boring. the one amendment this system makes is admitting `earth` into hue_scheme as a peer of the geometric schemes. no source lists it as a scheme, but every working illustrator knows it as one: users say "earthy" and mean a family of pigments, not a hue relationship. the axis serves the user's word, so earth is in.

## saturation and temperature

no source gives a usable discrete vocabulary for saturation or temperature. color theory teaching treats them as continuous quantities (percent saturation, warm/cool as a slider), which is honest and useless for a wrangler. the values in this folder's axes (muted / natural / saturated / accent, warm / cool / split / neutral) are this branch's own discretization, and the least externally-grounded part of the whole branch. expect them to move when real usage arrives. `accent` is included on the strength of how often illustration discourse names the single-pop structure ("the red coat problem") despite no textbook formalizing it.

## illustration-specific palette practice

sources on illustration palette selection (alvalyn, various illustration teaching) emphasize that working illustrators pick palettes as small fixed sets (3-5 hues) with a dominant hue and support hues, rather than via schemes. that matches the axis design here: canonicals should read like a small palette decision, not a color-wheel geometry. it also predicts the canonical set will end up bigger and messier than the scheme list, which is fine. canonicals follow images, not the wheel.

## open reconciliation

the engine already has a palette metric predating this branch. the palette folder must express itself as arguments to that operator. until that reconciliation pass, every `would_touch` hint in canonical_tags.md is a placeholder.
